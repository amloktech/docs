Complexity tiers reflect the full borrower-permissioned workload: connected bank accounts, connected business systems, total records analyzed, and analysis scope. They apply separately to Insights and Signal, so an account can have one underwriting tier and a different monitoring tier.

## Compare the tiers

Each tier covers a combined workload across banking and the business systems your borrower authorizes you to access.

| Workload dimension | Standard | Advanced | Complex |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Connected bank accounts | 2 | 4 | 8 |
| Business integrations, in addition to banking | 5 | 10 | 20 |
| Total new records analyzed / month | 75,000 | 250,000 | 1,000,000 |
| Analysis scope | Routine reconciliation and standard analysis | Multiple entities or channels and deeper reconciliation | Extensive relationships, exceptions, and investigation |

**Record volumes include all connected sources**, such as bank transactions, payment records, commerce orders, accounting records, and separately analyzed line items. The integration counts exclude banking connections.

These are tier workload profiles. Your order form confirms the applicable source connections, record allowances, historical lookback, and analysis scope. Evaluate the full workload: evidence quality, related entities, document volume, and reconciliation depth also matter. A high-volume dataset with consistent evidence can require less analysis than a smaller dataset with substantial exceptions.

The monthly volumes describe new activity for monitoring. Underwriting covers the agreed historical snapshot, as explained in [Underwriting history and monthly monitoring](#underwriting-history-and-monthly-monitoring).

These examples describe workload profiles used to plan the tiers. They are **not universal limits or automatic classification rules**.

## Standard business baseline

The Standard baseline connects **two bank accounts plus five business systems**. Count business systems separately from bank accounts, even when a bank-data connector supplies both accounts.

An illustrative five-system stack contains a payment processor, a commerce platform, an accounting and invoicing platform, an inventory system, and a payroll system. QuickBooks, Xero, and Wave are alternatives within the accounting slot. Invoicing inside the same accounting account is not an additional integration. Inventory and payroll fill the two additional slots for this estimate; they are not a claim that every business uses this stack or that every connector is enabled.

### Industry benchmark and order volume

[Littledata's October 2026 benchmark](https://www.littledata.io/benchmarks/average-order-value) reports a USD 111 median store average order value across 433 Shopify stores, measured from July 20 to October 17, 2026. Using the [Bank of Canada's 2025 annual average](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/annual-average-exchange-rates/) of CAD 1.3978 per USD gives CAD 155.1558 per order for this model.

`Monthly orders = annual sales in CAD / 12 / 155.1558`

| Annual sales | Estimated orders / month | Estimated new records / month | Estimated 24-month activity history |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| CAD 2M | 1,074 | About 20,000 | About 471,000 |
| CAD 3.5M | 1,880 | About 34,000 | About 806,000 |
| CAD 5M | 2,685 | About 47,000 | About 1.14M |

The record estimates combine the published order-value benchmark with the explicit processing assumptions below. They are not measured industry averages for five connected systems. The Shopify sample is not Canadian-only or matched to this revenue band. Assume even monthly sales, consistent tax and refund treatment, and a constant historical workload. The historical FX rate is a planning conversion, not a billing exchange rate.

### Records across the complete stack

This example uses CAD 3.5M annual sales and 1,880 monthly orders. All counts are rounded planning estimates; the unrounded model totals 33,568 records.

| Source | Records included in the modeled analysis | Estimated new records / month |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Two bank accounts, combined | Deposits, operating payments, payroll debits, transfers, taxes, and fees | 300 |
| Commerce, such as Shopify | Orders, distinct order lines, and fulfillment or shipment records | 7,500 |
| Payments, such as Stripe | Successful payments, processor balance transactions, adjustments, and payouts | 4,000 |
| One accounting platform | Invoices, accounting payments, journals, journal lines, and other accounting records | 17,400 |
| Inventory system | Stock issue movements, receiving, purchasing, and adjustment records | 4,200 |
| Payroll system | Pay-run, employee pay, and related records within the agreed scope | 100 |
| **Total** | **Two bank accounts plus five business systems** | **About 34,000** |

A sale can produce an order, order lines, a payment, a processor ledger entry, accounting postings, and inventory movements. These are linked records to analyze and reconcile. They remain one economic sale.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What counts as a record">
    For this workload model, a record is one distinct source object or one separately analyzed child row, such as an order line or journal line. Embedded scalar fields are not additional records. An invoice's embedded lines are not separately counted in this example; a larger document still affects processing complexity.

    Count each source record once in the relevant dataset. Preserve distinct evidence from different systems when it is needed for reconciliation, while linking the shared economic event. Do not count webhook retries, duplicate ingestion, or unchanged refetches as new records. Do not multiply records by API calls or refresh frequency.

    The monthly table covers newly generated activity records. Material changes to existing records, initial customer and product imports, documents, and other enabled resources require separate workload allowances. A refresh of the same unchanged stock balance is not a new inventory movement.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Record multipliers and assumptions">
    Let **N** be monthly orders, rounded to the nearest whole order. These multipliers are modeling assumptions informed by provider object structures, not published averages of SaaS records per business:

    - **Banking: 300 total entries.** Model 22 processor deposits, 100 supplier and operating payments, 40 payroll entries, and 138 other receipts, transfers, financing, tax, and fee entries across both accounts. Payroll or supplier batching can reduce this count. Bank payouts can settle many sales, as described in [Stripe's payout reconciliation guide](https://docs.stripe.com/reports/payout-reconciliation).
    - **Commerce: 4N.** One order, two distinct order lines, and one fulfillment or shipment record per order. [Shopify's order model](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/Order) exposes these relationships. Two distinct lines is a planning approximation: [Lebesgue's basket benchmarks](https://lebesgue.io/growth/ecommerce-benchmarks) show variation by sector, and product units do not necessarily equal distinct order lines.
    - **Payments: 2.1N + 44.** One successful payment and one processor balance transaction per sale; a 5% allowance for paired refund or adjustment records; and 22 payout objects with their 22 balance transactions. The 5% is a workload allowance, not a measured refund rate. [Stripe documents separate balance transactions](https://docs.stripe.com/reports/balance-transaction-types); ordinary fee amounts can be fields on those records rather than extra records. Model the actual payment channel, without adding a second payment stream for the same sales merely because Shopify Payments and Stripe are both mentioned.
    - **Accounting: 9N + 500.** A detailed invoice-and-settlement scenario with one invoice, one accounting payment, two journal headers, and five journal lines per order, plus 500 other accounting records including their separately analyzed rows. [Intuit documents accounting payments linked to invoices](https://developer.intuit.com/app/developer/qbo/docs/api/accounting/all-entities/payment), and [Xero exposes journals and journal lines where access is enabled](https://developer.xero.com/documentation/api/accounting/journals/). This model assumes those source records exist, are accessible, and are analyzed. Daily summary bookkeeping or cash-sale receipts can produce substantially fewer records; additional postings can produce more. Do not invent source journals to meet the estimate.
    - **Inventory: 2.2N + 100.** Two stock issue movements per order, a 10% movement allowance, and 100 purchasing or receiving records. The allowance is not a measured return rate. Reservation event streams, serial-number detail, and additional warehouses may require a larger scope.
    - **Payroll: 100.** A planning allowance for employee pay, pay-run, and related records. Detailed timesheets or attendance events require a separate allowance.

    Accounting representation is a major sensitivity: summarizing sales by day reduces the volume, while detailed journal and line analysis increases it. Five integrations alone do not predict record volume.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Lower-value orders and larger baskets">
    Littledata reports sector median order values of USD 70 for beauty and skincare, USD 85 for food and beverage, and USD 213 for home and furniture. With the same FX conversion and processing assumptions, the CAD 2M-5M range produces approximately:

    | Ecommerce benchmark | New records / month |
    | --- | ---: |
    | Beauty and skincare | 31,000-75,000 |
    | Food and beverage | 25,000-62,000 |
    | Home and furniture | 11,000-25,000 |

    These isolate the effect of order value while holding the assumed basket and processing structure constant. Sector-specific basket sizes, seasonality, bookkeeping, product catalogs, and the chosen fifth system can change the result further. They are sensitivity scenarios, not an observed distribution or a guaranteed upper bound.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How the record volumes scale

The Standard profile provides room above the approximately 20,000-50,000 records estimated using the all-store order-value benchmark and accommodates the lower-ticket sensitivity example. Advanced and Complex increase both connected-source capacity and record volume alongside the depth of analysis. These are AMLOK workload profiles, not industry-defined tier thresholds.

## Underwriting history and monthly monitoring

**Insights** analyzes the agreed historical snapshot. At the CAD 3.5M baseline, a steady 12-month history contains about **403,000** activity records; a 24-month history contains about **806,000**, before initial master-data imports and document evidence. Historical retrieval remains subject to source access and retention. For example, Shopify requires additional access for orders older than 60 days.

**Signal** processes new activity and agreed changes during the monitoring period. A 24-month initial import does not mean that the full history becomes new monthly volume. Define any full-history reassessment separately in the scope.

The billing units remain a completed [Insights underwriting](/pricing/insights) and an active [Signal account-month](/pricing/signal). Individual records do not each create a separate charge within the agreed scope. Consumer portfolios and non-commerce businesses use profiles appropriate to their sources and analysis.

## Set the limits in your order form

Specify the limits that apply to each contracted workflow:

| Input | What to agree |
| --- | --- |
| Application scope | Borrowers, businesses, guarantors, related entities, and facilities. |
| Source connections | Bank accounts and business-system accounts, counted separately. |
| Evidence volume | Documents, pages, total records across all sources, initial master data, changes, and historical lookback. |
| Analysis depth | Reconciliation, investigations, scenarios, and required outputs. |
| Monitoring scope | Active account definition, recurring volumes, cadence, and reassessment coverage. |
| Out-of-scope work | Approval process, applicable rate, and when a new underwriting is required. |

Agree the tier before billable work begins. If several dimensions exceed the scope, assess the workflow as a whole and agree the appropriate tier or custom price. There is no automatic surcharge based on internal model calls or retries.
