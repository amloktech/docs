Load **Explore sample businesses** during first-use setup, or choose **Dev Tools > Sandbox scenarios > Load six sample businesses**. The package creates six separate business journeys. Use a completed `healthy` journey to explore the full result, then use the other cases to test missing information and recovery.

## What each business contains

| Area | Included data | Where to inspect it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business and owner | Fictional registered business, industry, address, registration number, sole owner and director, and ownership relationship. | Borrower profile, verification source records and the risk package's `verification`. |
| Banking | Operating account and business card, sixty months of categorized activity, monthly statements and daily operating-account closing balances. | Connection data, bank statement and cash-flow dimensions. |
| Accounting | Five annual income statements, invoices, credit notes, paid and outstanding bills, balanced monthly cash-cost postings, current supporting schedules, two credit facilities and tax records. | Normalized records, financial dimensions and `analyses.financial_statements`. |
| Payments and commerce | Five years of individual orders and payments, customers, products, discounts, cancellations, declined payments, partial/full refunds, disputes, weekly payouts and explicit settlement links. | Source records, revenue metrics, concentrations and reconciliation. |
| KYB and owner verification | Simulated company registration, owner identity, liveness, ownership, screening and account-holder checks. | `verification`, its provenance and the associated reports. |
| Documents | Six readable PDFs: financial statement with five-year comparisons, sixty-month bank history, business registration, identity verification report, tax filing and lending terms. | Origination documents and the document endpoints. |
| Underwriting | Submitted application, versioned lending product and risk policy, assessment, dimensions, source evidence and analysis sections. | Origination drawer and immutable risk package. |
| Monitoring | Funded deal, baseline, evaluations, events and scenario-specific alerts. | Portfolio drawer and monitoring endpoints. |
| System records | API requests, scenario steps and assertions, exports, usage, complexity assessments, test funding, credit movements and statements. | Dev Tools, Billing and the corresponding API endpoints. |

Completed assessments create risk packages and monitoring records. An Origination that requires a document, requires reauthorization or fails retains that state; it does not invent completed results. Recovery scenarios continue the same workflow after the required action. A cancelled connection scenario intentionally creates no connected sources or document pack.

## Understand the figures

The source history covers **sixty complete calendar months before the run's creation month**. A run created in November 2026 contains business activity from November 1, 2021 through October 31, 2026. The history is fixed for that run, including later recovery or refresh actions. Leap days are included.

Balances and settlement statuses describe the snapshot at the history cutoff. A payout scheduled after that cutoff remains pending in this frozen example, even if you load or inspect it later. `extensions.sandbox.history_window` identifies that boundary; collection timestamps record when AMLOK generated and ingested the sample.

The underwriting observation window remains the **latest twelve complete months**. Five years of transactions are not added together and labeled annual revenue. Prior-year comparisons, current balances and pending processor settlements remain distinct. Retrieve the full historical collection through the data endpoints; the risk package freezes the observations used in its assessment.

Canadian businesses report in **CAD**; US businesses report in **USD**. Your simulated AMLOK wallet and API consumption remain **CAD**. Business revenue and financing currency do not change the currency of your AMLOK invoice.

### Follow the money

1. An order contains its items, discount, sales tax and total. Cancelled or declined orders have no captured funds.
2. A payment records the capture, processing fee and any refund. Refunds and lost disputes have their own dated records and linked accounting credit notes.
3. Processor balance entries group charges and later deductions into weekly payouts after a business-day availability delay. Each paid payout links to its bank transaction; pending payouts remain a processor receivable.
4. Supplier payments, payroll, rent, software, insurance, debt service, capital expenditure and simulated tax remittances affect the operating bank. Business-card repayments link both sides of the transfer.
5. Daily bank balances roll forward from posted activity. Annual statements use business revenue excluding sales tax, with expenses and processor fees accounted for separately.

**Bank receipts are not gross sales.** Taxes, fees, refunds, disputes and settlement timing explain the difference. Inspect `extensions.sandbox.balance_entries` on payouts for the signed allocations. The risk package’s reconciliation analysis compares explicit paid payouts with their bank postings. Its cash-flow statement uses actual operating-bank receipts and payments; a gross invoice total is not treated as net cash. Missing source connections leave links absent; a deterioration scenario intentionally changes bank collections and can produce unmatched reconciliation.

Amounts are decimal strings; ratios such as `"0.20"` mean 20%. Evidence references identify the collected record and field used in a calculation. Not every metric is applicable or available: unsupported bureau information, unobserved churn and calibrated predictive outputs remain unavailable.

A November 2026 run contains approximately **135,600 normalized source records**, including more than **21,800 orders** across the six businesses. Record counts depend on the run date and scenario. See the [example coverage manifest](/downloads/sandbox-history-example.json) for counts by record type and annual figures.

The profiles use fictional ticket sizes, growth and expense assumptions. Payment examples follow Stripe-style gross/fee/net and availability concepts; orders follow Shopify-style order, refund and customer relationships. These are synthetic normalized records, not records fetched from either provider. Shopify Payments is not assumed to be accessible through a separate Stripe account.

The illustrative card tariff is 2.9% + 0.30, with original fees retained after refunds. Tax rates and monthly remittance dates are fixed simulation assumptions, not tax calculations for a real jurisdiction. Weekends are excluded from settlement delays; public holidays are not modeled. Accounting journals are a balanced cash-cost subset, not a complete general ledger.

These broad references are not predictions for an individual business.

### Business history versus workspace activity

The businesses have five years of records. The workspace, API requests, invitations, verification checks, scenario actions and AMLOK billing records keep their **actual execution dates**. Loading samples does not fabricate five years of customer access or AMLOK charges. Scheduled future installments are explicitly marked as scheduled.

New sample runs use this history automatically. Existing runs retain their original data; use [Sandbox data setup](/console/sandbox) to replace a workspace's samples when you choose to. A reset clears Sandbox data and requires confirmation; loading the new catalog never resets a workspace silently.

Live history depends on the provider and consented permissions. For example, [Shopify requires additional access for orders older than sixty days](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/Order). Sandbox history is not a promise that every live connection supplies five years.

<Note>
The data, people, verification results and files are fictional. Identity files are **verification reports**, not genuine identity documents. A simulated passing check does not establish that any real person, business or document was verified.
</Note>

## Retrieve real document URLs

Document IDs are durable references; download URLs are temporary capabilities. **Do not hard-code a URL from a screenshot, example response or previous run.** Request a fresh link when the user opens a file and use its `expires_at` value.

1. Wait for `GET /v1/sandbox/runs/{id}` to return `completed`.
2. Find resources with `kind: "document"`, or read `document_ids` from the Origination's submitted request.
3. Read `GET /v1/documents/{id}` and check that `status` is `available`.
4. Call `POST /v1/documents/{id}/download-links` with a `purpose` describing the access and a new idempotency key.
5. Fetch the returned `data.url` without forwarding your API key. Check the file's SHA-256 against the returned `sha256`.

This Node.js 22+ example downloads one file from your own completed run. Set `AMLOK_DOCUMENT_ID` to a document resource ID; keep `AMLOK_API_KEY` server-side.

```javascript
import { randomUUID, createHash } from 'node:crypto';
import { writeFile } from 'node:fs/promises';

const base = process.env.AMLOK_API_BASE_URL || 'https://api.amlok.io';
const id = process.env.AMLOK_DOCUMENT_ID;
if (!id || !process.env.AMLOK_API_KEY?.startsWith('amlok_test_')) {
  throw new Error('Set a document ID and Sandbox API key.');
}
const response = await fetch(`${base}/v1/documents/${encodeURIComponent(id)}/download-links`, {
  method: 'POST', redirect: 'error', signal: AbortSignal.timeout(30000),
  headers: {
    Authorization: `Bearer ${process.env.AMLOK_API_KEY}`,
    'Content-Type': 'application/json',
    'Idempotency-Key': randomUUID(),
  },
  body: JSON.stringify({ purpose: 'Review fictional Sandbox evidence' }),
});
const body = await response.json();
if (!response.ok) throw new Error(`${response.status}: ${body.error?.message}`);
if (body.meta.environment !== 'sandbox') throw new Error('Expected Sandbox.');
const link = body.data;
if (Date.parse(link.expires_at) <= Date.now()) throw new Error('Request a fresh link.');
const download = await fetch(link.url, {
  redirect: 'error', signal: AbortSignal.timeout(30000),
});
if (!download.ok) throw new Error(`Download failed: ${download.status}`);
const bytes = Buffer.from(await download.arrayBuffer());
if (createHash('sha256').update(bytes).digest('hex') !== link.sha256) {
  throw new Error('File checksum mismatch.');
}
await writeFile('sandbox-document.pdf', bytes, { mode: 0o600 });
```

Treat signed URLs as secrets while valid. A URL can stop working after expiry, key revocation or an authorization change. Keep the document ID and request a new link when access is still authorized. See [files and downloads](/api-reference/downloads) for range requests and download errors.

## Follow the result back to its sources

From a completed Origination, retrieve its `risk_package_id`. Inspect `assessment`, then a dimension's `metrics`. An available metric includes its value, unit, calculation version, assumptions and inputs. Follow the input's `evidence_id` and `field_path` to the source observation; use `document_id` when a file supports that observation.

Use `GET /v1/data/records` to inspect normalized integration data and its source envelope. Traverse the returned pagination cursors to retrieve the whole collection. The package also includes financial statements, obligations and repayment schedules, collateral, tax, concentration, reconciliation and deterministic cash projections under `analyses`. Verification sections include the source report references and missing-field explanations.

The `manual_review` case deliberately omits the proposed repayment schedule, so DSCR and dependent debt-service metrics remain unavailable. The lending-terms PDF explains that omission. The `monitoring_alert` case reduces bank receipts while keeping the accounting observations, producing both deterioration and a reconciliation difference. `monitoring_recovery` restores receipts and reevaluates the monitor.

## Refresh samples without losing prior tests

The catalog version identifies the sample release. Fetch it from `GET /v1/sandbox/catalog` before starting a new run. Existing runs, documents and risk packages remain unchanged when the sample package is updated. **Load six sample businesses** or **Repeat with fresh records** creates new records with the current package; it does not rewrite old results or reset shared credits.

To discard all prior tests and load only a fresh package, an Owner uses **Workspaces > Sandbox data > Load fresh sample data**. To remove samples and start from your own requests, choose **Start empty** there. Both are destructive resets; review [reset scope and confirmation](/console/workspaces#reset-sandbox-data).

For expected outcomes, permissions and executable commands, see [businesses and scenarios](/sandbox/businesses-and-scenarios) and [API Sandbox journeys](/api-reference/sandbox-journeys). Use [simulated billing](/sandbox/simulated-billing) to reconcile funding and consumption. Initial sample preparation records events without sending webhooks; explicitly enable delivery to your test receiver when you want real HTTP delivery.
