Use this guide to connect your Amlok security review to the appropriate standards, laws, and regulatory guidance. Define the Insights or Signal service, data flows, jurisdiction, assessment period, and responsibility split before evaluating the supporting evidence.

A framework reference describes the subject of the review. A certification, independent assurance report, statutory registration, or accreditation has its own issuer, scope, validity, and evidence. Review those items separately through [due diligence](/security/due-diligence).

## Assurance and security frameworks

| Framework | Type | Control focus | Evidence to review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [SOC 2 Type II](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/landing/system-and-organization-controls-soc-suite-of-services) | Independent assurance report | Relevant Trust Services Criteria and control operation over a defined period. | System description, covered criteria, period, auditor opinion, exceptions, subservice organizations, and customer responsibilities. |
| [SOC 3](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-3/) | General-use assurance report | High-level assurance on applicable Trust Services Criteria. | Report identity, system and period, and the relationship to more detailed assurance materials. A SOC 3 report does not contain the detailed control testing of SOC 2. |
| [ISO/IEC 27001:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) | Information security management system standard | Risk assessment, information protection, access, suppliers, incidents, continuity, and improvement. | Certificate scope and validity where applicable, statement of applicability, risk treatment, and relevant operating evidence. |
| [ISO/IEC 42001:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) | AI management system standard | Accountable AI governance, risk and impact assessment, lifecycle controls, and improvement. | Management-system scope, intended uses, assigned responsibilities, assessments, and applicable assurance materials. |
| [AIUC-1](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/learn/about) | Voluntary AI agent assurance standard | Agent data/privacy, security, safety, reliability, accountability, and societal risk. | In-scope agents, standard version, control mapping, independent evaluation results, and assessment materials. See [AI governance](/security/ai-governance). |
| [PCI DSS](https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide) | Payment-card security standard | Protection of payment-card data within the applicable integration boundary. | Stripe service scope, Amlok's integration and applicable validation obligations, and the responsibility allocation. |
| [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) | Voluntary risk framework | Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover. | A scoped control mapping, risks, priorities, and operating evidence. |
| [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) | Voluntary AI risk framework | Governance, context, measurement, and management of AI risks, including relevant generative-AI guidance. | Intended-use assessment, evaluation design, limitations, oversight, and monitoring. |

### SOC 2 review areas

| Trust Services category | Deployment questions |
| --- | --- |
| Security | How are access, tenant boundaries, changes, vulnerabilities, and incidents controlled? |
| Availability | Which dependencies, recovery procedures, and service commitments are included? |
| Processing integrity | How are evidence, transformations, exceptions, and assessment outputs checked? |
| Confidentiality | What limits apply to access, transmission, suppliers, retention, and deletion? |
| Privacy | How are permitted purposes, collection authority, rights, and breach responsibilities defined? |

The criteria and controls covered by a particular report determine what it establishes. Do not assume that every report covers every category or every Amlok deployment.

## Canadian privacy and financial frameworks

| Framework | Type | Control focus | Evidence to review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PIPEDA | Federal privacy law | Accountable processing, appropriate purposes, safeguards, rights, and breach handling. | Data-flow and purpose records, contractual instructions, rights processes, and breach procedures. |
| Québec Law 25 | Amendments to provincial privacy law | Governance, impact assessments, sensitive data, exclusively automated decisions, and transfers. | Applicable assessments, notices, explanation/review procedures, and transfer arrangements. |
| Alberta PIPA | Provincial privacy law | Reasonable collection/use, safeguards, rights, and service-provider transparency. | Provincial scope, privacy practices, cross-border service information, and breach assessment. |
| British Columbia PIPA | Provincial privacy law | Private-sector processing, protection, access, correction, and retention. | Applicable organization/activity, permissions, records, and rights procedures. |
| OSFI B-10 | Supervisory guidance | Third-party risk, contracts, monitoring, concentration, and exit. | Service dependencies, supplier review, contract terms, continuity, and exit assistance. |
| OSFI B-13 | Supervisory guidance | Technology governance, cyber security, operations, and resilience. | Relevant service controls, incident coordination, and security evidence. |
| OSFI E-23 | Supervisory guidance | Model inventory, validation, monitoring, accountability, and change. | Model and use-case documentation, limitations, validation and monitoring evidence. |
| OSFI E-21 | Supervisory guidance | Critical operations and operational resilience. | Dependency mapping, recovery arrangements, scenario testing, and incident processes. |
| Canadian sanctions | Laws and regulations | Restricted parties/activities, ownership and control, and applicable reporting. | Screening scope, escalation, review records, and applicable legal restrictions. |
| PCMLTFA / FINTRAC | Statutory AML/ATF framework | Covered reporting entities' identification, beneficial ownership, monitoring, records, and reporting. | Entity/activity determination and the agreed verification and evidence responsibilities. |

Read [Canadian requirements](/security/canada) and [Québec privacy](/security/quebec) for official sources and applicability. Laws and supervisory guidance are not certifications. Institutions retain their own legal responsibilities when using a service provider.

## Open banking and data sharing

| Framework | Type | Control focus | Evidence to review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canada's consumer-driven banking framework | Statutory framework and implementing requirements | Participation, permissioned data access, responsibilities, and oversight. | Applicable participant/provider status, permitted services, consent, and final requirements. |
| Financial Data Exchange / FDX API | Technical data-sharing standard | Interoperable data formats, secure authorization and exchange, and consent management. | Implemented API/profile version, provider scope, permissions, revocation, and applicable conformance evidence. |

FDX's designation as Canada's technical standards body is distinct from an individual participant's accreditation or an implementation's certification. See [Open banking and FDX](/security/open-banking-and-fdx) for current Canadian status and a connection responsibility matrix.

## United States requirements

| Area | Relevant framework | Evidence focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Financial data protection | GLBA and applicable safeguards rules | Security program, service-provider terms, access, encryption, incident coordination, and legal roles. |
| Credit decisions | ECOA / Regulation B | Actual decision factors, fair-lending review, explanations, notices, and retention. |
| Consumer reporting | FCRA and relevant state laws | Agency, furnisher and user roles; permissible purpose, accuracy, disputes, and notices. |
| Privacy and automated decisions | CCPA / CPRA and applicable state AI/privacy requirements | Processing roles, exemptions, rights, assessments, explanations, and oversight. |
| Financial-institution vendors | NYDFS Part 500 and interagency third-party guidance | Contractual controls, supplier evidence, monitoring, resilience, and incident notification. |
| Predictive model governance | Federal Reserve SR 26-2 | Applicable model scope, validation, monitoring, and limitations. Generative and agentic AI are outside this guidance's scope. |
| Sanctions | OFAC requirements | Applicable U.S. nexus, program restrictions, screening, escalation, and records. |

Use [United States requirements](/security/united-states) for scope and primary sources. A customer's regulated status does not automatically make every vendor subject to every rule that governs the customer.

## Azure and Stripe responsibilities

Amlok uses Microsoft Azure for its platform and Stripe for billing payments. Provider assurance covers the services and systems listed in the provider's reports. Amlok remains responsible for its application, data handling, access decisions, configuration, and contractual obligations. Your institution remains responsible for its permitted use of data and lending decisions.

Review [hosting and subprocessors](/security/hosting-and-subprocessors), [platform security](/security/platform-security), and [privacy and data handling](/security/privacy). Stripe billing payments are separate from institution lending, borrower repayment, and funds movement. The Amlok product API exposes credit and consumption reporting rather than payment, payout, transfer, or refund commands.

## Request evidence for your service

Specify the service boundary, framework, report period or standard version, customer obligations, and decisions your team needs to support. Request the applicable materials through [due diligence](/security/due-diligence). Review confidential materials under their access terms; provider reports and framework descriptions do not substitute for Amlok-specific evidence.
