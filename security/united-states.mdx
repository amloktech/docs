title: "United States requirements"
description: "Financial data safeguards, credit decisions, state privacy, and sensitive-data requirements."
---

For a United States deployment, determine the requirements by legal entity, regulator, state, activity, and information involved.

## Financial information safeguards

The Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and the applicable regulator's rules govern relevant financial information. The FTC Safeguards Rule applies to financial institutions within the FTC's jurisdiction; other regulated institutions may have different implementing requirements. Covered institutions must address service-provider safeguards and oversight. See the [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know).

Review the security program, risk assessment, access protection, encryption, monitoring, testing, incident procedures, and contractual safeguards applicable to the service. Agree evidence and responsibilities for the deployment.

## Lending decisions and explanations

The Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B govern relevant credit decisions and notices. Where a statement of adverse-action reasons is required, it must identify the specific principal reasons. A score or generic reference to an algorithm does not replace the actual reasons. See [Regulation B, section 1002.9](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/9/).

Establish approved criteria, assess discrimination risk, verify explanations against the actual decision process, and maintain appropriate records. AMLOK outputs must be used within that approved scope. See [AI and model governance](/security/ai-governance).

## Consumer reporting

If a workflow obtains, supplies, or uses consumer reports, assess the Fair Credit Reporting Act and each party's role. Permissible purpose, accuracy and dispute duties, and relevant adverse-action or risk-based-pricing notices depend on the activity. See the [FTC FCRA overview](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/fair-credit-reporting-act) and [notice guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-credit-decisions-what-know-about-adverse-action-risk-based-pricing-notices).

Do not assume alternative data or a business-lending label removes consumer-reporting obligations. Assess information and actual use, including personal guarantees.

## State privacy and breach obligations

Map privacy, security, and notification laws applicable to the people, data, and entities involved. Where the CCPA applies, address rights, notices, service-provider terms, and sensitive information. Assess exemptions at the appropriate scope rather than assuming every record of a financial business is exempt. See the [California Attorney General's guidance](https://www.oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa).

State-specific automated-decision, consumer-credit, biometric, and other requirements may need additional analysis. A national statement does not replace a state-by-state deployment assessment.

## Health and biometric information

HIPAA applies to covered entities and business associates within its scope. Processing protected health information for a covered entity may require a business associate agreement and applicable safeguards. See [HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html).

Do not send protected health information, biometric templates, or verification media through an ordinary financial-data workflow without first agreeing purpose, legal requirements, supported scope, safeguards, and retention. An external verification provider's test results or assurance reports do not apply to AMLOK's own service.
